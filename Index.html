Detection Capabilities

What the Sensor Data Reveals

Bearing defects — inner race, outer race, ball fault signatures
Mechanical imbalance — mass distribution anomalies in rotating components
Misalignment — angular and parallel shaft misalignment patterns
Mechanical looseness — structural and component looseness signatures
Gear wear — tooth mesh frequency anomalies
Resonance — natural frequency excitation in mounting structures
Motor electrical faults — rotor bar defects, air gap eccentricity
Cavitation — pump and compressor cavitation signatures


Beyond Downtime

What Else the Data Captures

Energy Waste Detection
A motor running with early bearing wear draws excess current for weeks before failure. Vibration and current data together identify motors burning kilowatt-hours unnecessarily on every shift — a cost that never appears on a maintenance work order.
Product Quality Correlation
Equipment operating outside its vibration baseline is often simultaneously operating outside its process specification. Facilities consistently find their defect rate correlated directly with condition data they were already collecting.
Capital Planning Precision
Condition data replaces arbitrary replacement schedules with evidence-based end-of-life curves. CAPEX decisions get made on documented equipment behavior — not on calendar dates or whoever argues loudest in the budget meeting.
Institutional Knowledge Capture
When your best technician transfers, gets promoted, or leaves — their pattern recognition walks out with them. A documented failure signature library built over time captures what they knew as searchable, transferable data. The knowledge stays when they don't.
